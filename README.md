# Seasonal Campaign Launch Readiness Plan

> **Illustrative portfolio exercise.** This plan uses public information about The Home Depot's 2026 Spring Starts event and Marketing Project Manager role. It is my independent work, not a description of The Home Depot's internal plans, systems, performance, or team practices. All statuses and targets below are hypothetical.

## The challenge

The Home Depot's Spring Starts promotion ran March 19-April 1, 2026 across stores, homedepot.com, and the mobile app. A campaign with that footprint can only launch predictably when Strategy, Media, Creative/Agency, Product, IT, and channel teams share the same dependencies, approval dates, and escalation path.

**Objective:** create one operating view that makes ownership explicit, exposes risk early, and supports a fact-based go/no-go decision without taking functional ownership away from expert teams.

## My operating model

I would combine four lightweight controls:

1. **RACI for delivery:** one accountable owner and named contributors for every deliverable.
2. **DACI for decisions:** one driver, one approver, a deadline, and a recorded outcome for every launch-critical decision.
3. **Stage gates for readiness:** teams advance on evidence, not a subjective percent-complete update.
4. **RAID log for intervention:** risks, assumptions, issues, and dependencies each have an owner, trigger, response, and due date.

| Gate | Timing | Evidence required to exit |
|---|---:|---|
| **G0 — Align** | T-30 | Approved brief, audience, offers, KPIs, channel scope, budget guardrails |
| **G1 — Commit** | T-21 | Integrated plan, asset/media matrix, technical requirements, dependency owners, approval calendar |
| **G2 — Validate** | T-7 | Final creative, media trafficking, offer/content parity, web/app/store QA, tracking validation |
| **G3 — Launch** | T-2 | No open critical defects, accepted residual risks, monitoring and rollback plans, named go/no-go approver |
| **G4 — Learn** | T+7 | Results, launch incidents, lessons learned, and improvement actions with owners and dates |

## Integrated delivery view

| Deliverable | Accountable owner | Critical dependencies | Readiness check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Campaign brief and KPI tree | Strategy | Customer insight, business goal, offer strategy | Scope and success measures approved at G0 |
| Offer/channel source of truth | Merchandising / Channel Ops | Price, inventory, geography, legal terms | Same offer and dates verified across store, web, and app |
| Integrated plan, RAID, decision log | Marketing PMO | Dates and owners from every workstream | Critical path baselined; no milestone without an owner |
| Asset matrix and final creative | Creative / Agency & Studio | Brief, offers, specs, claims, review deadlines | Required variants approved and version-locked |
| Media plan and trafficking | Media | Budget, audience, approved assets, destination URLs | Placements, tags, flight dates, and fallbacks validated |
| Web, app, and store readiness | Product / IT / Channel Ops | Final assets, releases, catalog data, inventory | Cross-device and channel QA passed; rollback owner named |
| Measurement and monitoring | Analytics + Product / IT | KPI definitions, tagging, data latency, dashboard | Test events reconcile; launch-day alert thresholds agreed |

**Approval rules:** consolidate feedback into one review round per stage; assign one final approver; time-box launch-critical decisions to 24 hours; and require an impact assessment for changes after G2. The PMO owns orchestration and transparency; functional owners retain quality and approval authority.

## Three risks I would manage first

| Early-warning signal | Likely impact | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Creative approval misses its SLA or exceeds two revision rounds | Trafficking, localization, and QA compress onto the critical path | Run parallel specialist reviews, consolidate comments through one decision maker, and activate pre-approved fallback assets at the cutover time |
| Offer, inventory, or dates differ across store, web, and app | Customer confusion, rework, and avoidable service contacts | Maintain one versioned offer matrix, require three-channel owner sign-off, and block G3 until high-severity mismatches are resolved |
| App release, landing-page change, or tracking validation slips | A channel may launch late or performance may be unmeasurable | Validate in staging by T-14, reserve a release window, define rollback/manual measurement, and escalate any blocked critical-path item after 24 hours |

## Sample T-7 leadership dashboard

| Leading indicator | Illustrative status | Trigger for action |
|---|---:|---|
| Weighted launch readiness | 🟠 82% | Red if any required G2 evidence is missing at T-5 |
| Critical-path milestones complete | 19 / 22 | Escalate any slip with downstream impact |
| Blocked deliverables | 2; one blocked >48h | Escalate after 24h without a recovery owner/date |
| Pending decisions | 3; one overdue | DACI approver decides or delegates within 24h |
| Open critical / high defects | 0 / 2 | No-go for any open critical defect |
| G3 forecast | **Conditional go** | Convert to go only when named conditions close |

From T-14 through launch, I would use a 15-minute daily critical-path review, a twice-weekly leadership dashboard, and exception-based escalation. At T+7, the team would convert the retrospective into owned process changes—for example, a reusable proofing workflow, revised approval SLA, or new launch-readiness template.

## Why this approach fits the role

The role calls for end-to-end project management, cross-functional accountability, process improvement, and reporting. This plan makes each visible: a shared plan for delivery, explicit ownership for handoffs, gates and escalation for risk, and leading indicators that help leaders act before a deadline slips.

For an interview, I would keep this hypothetical case separate from a factual Flipkart example: **situation → cross-functional dependency → action I personally owned → measured result → lesson applied here**. That preserves credibility while showing that the operating judgment is grounded in real experience.
